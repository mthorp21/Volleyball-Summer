Sports PT insight · eccentric loadingThe slow lowering phase of a squat is called eccentric loading, and it generates greater mechanical stress on the muscle than the lifting phase. In sports physical therapy, eccentric exercise is the first-line treatment for patellar tendinopathy and Achilles tendinopathy — both common in volleyball athletes. This is why 3 seconds down is not just a coaching cue. It is the actual mechanism of adaptation.

Sports PT insight · rotator cuff anatomyThe rotator cuff is four muscles: supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, and subscapularis. Band external rotation targets infraspinatus and teres minor specifically. These two muscles decelerate the arm after every swing. In overhead athletes, weakness here is the leading cause of labral tears and rotator cuff impingement. Sport PTs call this the deceleration complex — and it is the structure most commonly damaged in volleyball hitters.

Sports PT insight · kinetic chainThe volleyball spike generates force through a kinetic chain: ankle plantarflexion → knee extension → hip extension → trunk rotation → shoulder internal rotation → wrist snap. A weakness anywhere in that chain transfers load to the next joint. This is why ankle stability and glute strength directly affect hitting power — not just injury risk. The chain is only as strong as its weakest link.

Sports PT insight · progressive overload and growth platesProgressive overload is the foundational principle of strength adaptation. Your body rebuilds stronger in response to stress — but only if the stress is applied gradually. Increasing load by more than 10 percent per week overwhelms tissue adaptation speed, which is how stress fractures develop. At 16, the growth plates at the ends of your long bones are still active. Form-first loading protects them.

Sports PT insight · stretch-shortening cyclePlyometric training works through the stretch-shortening cycle (SSC). When you land, muscles and tendons store elastic energy like a compressed spring. The faster you redirect that energy into the next jump, the more power output you produce. This is called reactive strength. Sport PTs measure it with a drop jump test — the ratio of jump height to ground contact time. It is the primary physical quality separating high-vertical from low-vertical athletes of equal strength.

Sports PT insight · 8RM and adolescent loadingAn 8-rep maximum (8RM) represents approximately 80 percent of your 1-rep max. This loading zone drives strength and hypertrophic adaptation while remaining safe for adolescent athletes.
